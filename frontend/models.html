How do these models work?

Last month assumes the next month will be as bumpy as the last one. Long run uses the average of all history so far. EWMA averages recent days with more weight on the latest. GARCH is a statistical model in which a big move raises expected volatility, which then fades back towards a long-run level. HAR is a regression on the last day, week and month. The three machine-learning models (ridge regression, gradient boosting and a small neural network) learn from all ten markets at once, using inputs such as recent volatility over different windows, the size of recent falls and the US market's mood. They are trained only on months that had already finished, and refitted every quarter.

Method and limits

  • Five years of daily prices. The first 500 days only train the models, which leaves about three years of out-of-sample forecasts per market. That is enough to compare models roughly, but close rankings can change with a different period.
  • A p-value is the chance of a difference this large from ordinary days. With 20 tests, about one would fall below 0.05 by luck alone.
  • A good risk forecast says how bumpy the ride may be, not which way prices will go.
