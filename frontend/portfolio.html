Would the "optimal" mix really have worked?

Why do optimised portfolios often disappoint?

The optimiser treats past average returns as if they were known facts, but five years of returns are a very noisy estimate of the future. It piles into whatever happened to do best, which is often just luck. This is the same overfitting problem seen in machine learning: a model that fits past data perfectly can do badly on new data. Volatility and correlations are more stable than returns, which is why lowest-risk portfolios usually hold up better out of sample. A simple equal mix is a surprisingly tough benchmark.
