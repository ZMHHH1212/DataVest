What is a moving average?

A moving average is the average closing price over the last 50 or 200 trading days. It smooths out daily noise so the trend is easier to see. When the price sits above its 200-day average, the market is usually described as being in an uptrend. Treat crossings as descriptions of what has happened, not predictions.

Falls from the peak

How far below its previous high the price was. Zero means a new high.

Momentum (RSI 14)

Compares the size of recent gains with recent losses on a 0–100 scale.

How is RSI used?

Above 70 is often called "overbought" and below 30 "oversold". These are rules of thumb: DataVest's own tests on the Models page found RSI signals no better than chance.

What is volatility?

How much the price jumps around, scaled to a year. Around 15–20% is normal for a broad stock index; single companies are often 30% or more.

What is a drawdown?

The fall from a high point to the following low. The worst one answers: if you had bought at the worst moment, how far down would you have been?

–

Why "per year"?

The compound annual growth rate is the steady yearly rate that turns the start value into the end value. It hides the bumps, so read it next to volatility and the worst fall.

What is Value at Risk?

The daily fall that was beaten only on 1 day in 20. Expected shortfall is the average fall on those worst days.
