What is volatility?

Volatility measures how much the price jumps around from day to day, scaled to a year. Around 15–20% is normal for a broad stock index. Single companies are often 30% or more. Higher volatility means bigger swings both up and down, so you need to be able to sit through drops without panicking.

What is a drawdown?

A drawdown is how far the price fell from its highest point before recovering. The maximum drawdown is the worst of these. It answers a practical question: if you had bought at the worst possible moment, how much would you have been down on paper?

Why "per year"?

The compound annual growth rate (CAGR) is the steady yearly rate that would turn the starting value into the ending one. It makes periods of different lengths comparable. It hides the bumps, which is why you should read it next to volatility and drawdown.
